## Understand the idea

A menu is a decision problem. Hick–Hyman-style findings relate choice time to uncertainty in controlled tasks.

They do not prove that every product should hide options or use exactly a certain number of buttons. Familiarity, grouping, labels, and task frequency matter.

Support recognition instead of forcing recall: show the available statuses, recent queues, or selected filters. Working memory is limited, but “seven items” is not a universal navigation rule.

Reduce the information a person must carry across steps.

Progressive disclosure puts common decisions first and exposes less common detail when needed. It works only if people can find the extra options and understand what is hidden.

An “Advanced” drawer should not conceal a setting most users need to finish the basic task.
