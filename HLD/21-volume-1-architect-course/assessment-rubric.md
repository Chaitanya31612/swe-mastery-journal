# Assessment rubric

Score eight dimensions 0–3 each, total 24: requirements; scale; data/interfaces; end-to-end flows; correctness/consistency; failure/operations/security; trade-offs/evolution; communication/adaptation.

For every dimension: 0 = missing or fundamentally wrong; 1 = terminology or partial reasoning requiring substantial help; 2 = a workable, justified mechanism without prompting; 3 = also handles a counterexample or changed constraint. Attach concrete evidence from the attempt. Reasonable alternatives are acceptable when assumptions and consequences are explicit.

An unaided module gate needs **18/24**, every dimension at least 2, and no broken central invariant. Do not award passing status merely for MCQs, matching the book, number of boxes, or an app-generated score. Early baseline scores identify gaps rather than pass/fail. A correctness guarantee stronger than the proposed mechanism can deliver is a critical defect even if the diagram looks plausible.

For a module, complete P1, P2, the repair when supplied, and the checkpoint. Explain at least three of four checkpoint answers including why a tempting distractor fails. Then complete a delayed altered-constraint explanation seven or more days later. An assisted attempt can complete practice but cannot establish independence. If a local module gate uses a focused mini-design, mark irrelevant rubric dimensions unassessed and use a later full mock to establish the overall standard; do not invent scores for absent evidence.

For the foundation checkpoint, retrieve the framework without notes, redesign an anchor, and attempt an unfamiliar read-heavy or admission-control system. Record the achieved level: practice completed, foundation demonstrated, or independent transfer demonstrated.

For course graduation, submit three unfamiliar 45-minute designs from different families, one changed requirement per round, and one real-system architecture review. At least two rounds must pass 18/24 with all dimensions at least 2; the remaining round receives a focused repair and later reattempt. The real review must distinguish observed code/infrastructure from inference and proposals. Obtain a peer review where possible; AI/self evaluation remains provisional.

Failure recovery, authentication/authorization, cost, and evolution should be relevant to the problem, not a recited checklist. For example, large media delivery needs egress/caching analysis; an inventory claim needs atomicity; chat needs durable acceptance and reconnect semantics.

Record completion as: “I demonstrated [level] through [unaided artifacts and delayed attempts]; my remaining gaps are [specific mechanisms].” This is evidence of skill at the course scope, not a promise of any interview outcome.
